## The typo that started it all

I was trying to type very fast, **without looking at the screen**. You know, to feel cool and keyboard-god-like.

It did not go well.

Typos everywhere. And the funniest part? **The spaces were the first thing to disappear.** Words just kept smashing into each other — `horseariding`, `sunflower`, `thunderstorm` — my brain knew exactly what I meant, but the text was one long, breathless run-on.

And here's the weird thing: *reading it back, I understood every word instantly.* My brain filled in the missing spaces without asking. It was like the words were still separate to me, even though the spaces were gone.

So I thought: what if I hide something *else* in that gap — something the brain can't auto-complete?

## The game idea

Meet [VowerSplit](/games/vowelsplit): two words merge with a hidden vowel in between. Your job is to spot it and type it.

`horseariding` → the missing letter is **a**. Type it. Next word. Ten seconds on the clock.

The trick is that a missing *space* is the one thing your brain happily repairs on its own — but a missing *letter* is where the illusion breaks. Suddenly you have to actually *read* the merged word, letter by letter, instead of letting autopilot handle it.

That's the whole game: **your own brain is the difficulty.**

## Three levels of brain-strain

Because "easy to learn" is nice, but games need a climb:

- **Easy** — 2 words, 1 gap. You get your bearings.
- **Medium** — 3 words, 2 gaps. The same vowel fills every gap, so once you spot one, you have the answer.
- **Hard** — 4 to 5 words, 3 to 4 gaps. The string gets long and your eyes have to work for it.

Same rule at every level: one vowel fills every gap between the words. Type it and the words split apart. The bigger the mess, the harder the hunt. It's a tiny little puzzle of attention — and it feels *great* when your eye locks onto the gap instantly.

## Why it's fun

I like games that use what you're already doing — typing — and turn your own habits against you. VowerSplit makes you slow down just enough to actually see the text. It's a 10-second sprint that's secretly a reading game.

Plus, it's oddly satisfying when the rhythm clicks: type, spot, type, spot, and the score counter pops up each time your finger finds the right key.

## It started with Spanish

A few days ago I wrote about [turning Tidy into a Spanish vocabulary tool](/blog/Tidy_Spanish_20261020). The problem was simple: Duolingo had given me the grammar basics, but I didn't know enough words to understand real sentences. [Tidy en español](/games/tidy-es) clears a pile of the 3,000 most common Spanish words, shows an English meaning for each, and remembers which ones you've already cleared.

It's still an experiment, and I'm still testing whether it helps. But the structure held up: a calm room, a pile of words, repetition without a timer. That doesn't depend on Spanish.

## So, Japanese

[Tidy 日本語](/games/tidy-ja) is the same game with 3,000 common Japanese words. The same things carry over:

- **No clock.** Pick a room of 30 to 500 words and work through it.
- **The room narrows.** The same few words come around again and again as the pile empties, which is the repetition vocabulary needs.
- **Meaning on every clear.** When a word clears you see the kanji, its kana reading, the romaji you typed and a short English meaning: `森 (もり)  mori  =  forest`.
- **Progress is remembered.** Words you've cleared are saved in your browser, and new rooms start with words you haven't met yet.

## What's different about Japanese

Japanese isn't typed the way Spanish is, so the game works a little differently.

- **You type romaji.** Each card shows the word in kanji (or kana) with its romaji underneath. You type the romaji to clear it. That means you learn the reading and see the characters, but you don't practise writing them.
- **More than one spelling is accepted.** Japanese romaji has several common spellings, so `shi` and `si`, `chi` and `ti`, `tsu` and `tu`, and `fu` and `hu` all work.
- **A different word list.** I used the open JMdict dictionary, picking its everyday-tagged words first and then the most frequent ones in written Japanese. The honest caveat: that list leans toward news and formal writing, so it includes words like *ratification* alongside *forest*. I'd like to improve it with a more conversational source later.

The word data comes from [JMdict](https://www.edrdg.org/jmdict/j_jmdict.html), from the Electronic Dictionary Research and Development Group, used under their licence.

## What it won't do

Same limits as the Spanish version. It gives you exposure, repetition, readings and meanings. It won't teach grammar, listening or kanji writing. It complements a course instead of replacing one.

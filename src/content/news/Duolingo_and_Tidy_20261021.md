I've used Duolingo for years. It taught me enough to see how sentences are put together: word order, verb forms, the little patterns that make a language click. But I kept hitting the same wall. I understood the sentence, yet a few words in it just wouldn't stick.

[Tidy](/games/tidy) started as an experiment to fix that. You type a word, at your own speed, and it shows you the meaning. There are no streaks, no hearts, and no timer. Nothing tells you to hurry.

This isn't a "Tidy vs. Duolingo" story. They do different jobs.

- **Duolingo** gives you structure. It builds grammar through guided lessons, and its streaks and leaderboards keep you coming back. That pressure works for a lot of people, and for years it worked for me.
- **Tidy** gives you room. It's for the words that won't stay in your head, practised casually, with no one watching and nothing to lose.

Duolingo can also be stressful. I get why it's built that way, since a little peer pressure pushes many people to do better. I just wanted to learn at my own pace, so I made something for that.

## What is Tidy?

Tidy is a typing game about clearing a messy room. A pile of words is scattered on the screen, and you type them away one at a time. There's no clock and no countdown, so nothing punishes you for pausing. Take a breath, sip your tea, and come back to it.

You choose how big the job is, anywhere from 30 to 500 words, the way you'd decide whether to tidy one shelf or the whole room. The pile is always on screen, so you can see how much is left without checking a score.

Most games get harder as you go. Tidy gets easier. When you clear a word, it's only replaced by a copy of a word still on screen, so the vocabulary shrinks as you play. By the end you're down to the last few words, and the final one falls into place. Then the room is empty, and the game gives you a quiet moment to look at what you just finished before you pick the next round.

In the language versions, every word you clear shows its meaning. By the end of a round you've typed each word several times with the meaning right next to it, and that repetition is what makes the words stay.

## Which one should you use?

If you want a push, Duolingo is great. If you just want to sit down and quietly pick up the words you keep forgetting, try Tidy. Using both works well too: Duolingo for how the language fits together, Tidy for the words that fill it in.
